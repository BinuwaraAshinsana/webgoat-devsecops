NOTE ON THE FIX
  MD5 is an unsalted, broken hash: MD5("password") is reversed instantly with a
  precomputed rainbow table. The endpoint now hashes the secret with bcrypt
  (BCryptPasswordEncoder), which salts every digest - so identical inputs give
  different hashes and precomputed tables are useless - and is deliberately slow.
  The exploit is genuinely blocked, not merely re-labelled: the same rainbow-table
  lookup that recovered the MD5 plaintext returns nothing against the bcrypt hash.

RESIDUAL RISK (documented for honesty)
  This lesson's secret is always one of five publicly-known words. bcrypt defeats
  the demonstrated exploit - a precomputed rainbow-table lookup - which the after
  screenshot confirms (CrackStation returns "unrecognized hash format", not found).
  The lesson can still be solved by brute-forcing the five known candidates through
  bcrypt.matches(), but that is a small-keyspace weakness of the lesson design, not
  of the hash function; no hash algorithm protects a secret drawn from five known
  words. The CWE-328 finding (use of MD5) is fully resolved.
